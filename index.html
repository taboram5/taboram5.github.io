Case study · Mental wellness

Serenity

Guided meditations, generated in the moment and spoken aloud.

Role
UI Developer, plus backend work
Team
4 people
Timeline
Jan–Apr 2026
Tools
React Native, OpenAI API, ElevenLabs API

Overview

Serenity is a mental wellness app built to support students during high-stress moments, including crises like school shootings. It brings together a guided meditation companion, a Tetris-based distraction game, and a library of crisis resources. Its companion, Seren, asks how you're feeling, writes a meditation for that moment, and reads it to you.

The problem

Stress and anxiety rarely arrive at a convenient time, and most wellness apps answer with a library of generic content to scroll through. We wanted support that meets people where they are, without the app pretending to be a therapist.

Key decisions

Decision 1

A chatbot that guides, not one that advises

Mental health chatbots can easily overstep by offering advice nobody asked for, which can be unhelpful or even harmful. I scoped Seren to one job: listen to how the user feels and generate a meditation for that moment. That kept the experience supportive while respecting the limits of what an app should do.

Decision 2

Voice over text

Reading a meditation is very different from being guided through one. I used ElevenLabs to turn each generated meditation into spoken audio. That made it more immersive, and more accessible for people who have trouble reading a screen, like users with low vision or anyone too overwhelmed to focus on text.

Decision 3

A calm visual language

Someone opening Serenity may already be overwhelmed, so the interface asks for as little attention as possible: a soft teal palette, rounded cards, generous spacing, and only a few choices per screen. I replaced the original emoji with a set of simple line icons so the app feels consistent and quiet.

The goal was never to fix how someone feels. It was to give them one calm next step.

What I built

I built the app's screens in React Native, including Home, Chat, Profile, and Resources, and connected Seren to the OpenAI and ElevenLabs APIs so a typed feeling becomes a spoken meditation. I coordinated features with my team through GitHub pull requests.

Reflection

Next, I'd test the meditation flow with students to learn whether the voice, pacing, and length actually help in stressful moments. I'd also add clearer paths from Seren to professional and emergency resources, so the app always knows when to hand off.

Overview

Lost & Hound is a live App Store app and website where Northeastern students post items they've lost. Other students keep an eye out around campus and contact the owner to return anything they find.

The problem

The original interface was built quickly with AI-generated code. It works, but it feels unpolished, and it wasn't designed with accessibility in mind. An app like this only works when lots of students use it, so every bit of friction or exclusion costs it reach.

My approach

  1. Audit. Review every screen against accessibility standards: contrast, text size, tap targets, labels for screen readers, and how deep the navigation goes.
  2. Simplify. Streamline layouts and navigation so the three key actions (posting an item, browsing, and contacting an owner) take fewer steps.
  3. Polish. Build a consistent visual system of type, color, spacing, and reusable components, then code it into the live app in JavaScript.

Before and after

This project is in progress. Side-by-side comparisons of the original and redesigned screens will appear here as each one ships.

What's next

Finish the remaining screens, run quick usability tests with students, and measure how many taps the key tasks take before and after the redesign.

El Apagón

The story

Puerto Rico has lived through repeated, long-lasting blackouts, and government neglect has left the problem unresolved. El Apagón shows that failure while centering what it reveals about the Puerto Rican people: their resilience.

My role

I made the project entirely on my own, from the concept and storyboard to the visual design and the code. The animation is built in p5.js, which let me control light, timing, and motion directly in code.

When the power goes out, the island doesn't go dark. People light it themselves.
